Common problems

Common washer problems we repair

From a drum that won’t drain to a door that won’t unlock, these are the washer problems we fix most often.

Washer won’t drain

A clogged drain pump filter, a sock caught in the pump, a kinked drain hose or a failed pump motor usually leave water in the drum.

Won’t spin or clothes come out soaked

Often the same drain problem, or a worn drive belt, a failed lid switch or door lock, a bad motor coupling or a worn clutch.

Leaking water

Common sources are a torn door boot on front-loaders, loose inlet hoses, a cracked tub or a leaking pump seal.

Shaking or walking during spin

An unbalanced load is the easy cause; worn shock absorbers, suspension springs or drum bearings are the repair causes.

Won’t start or turn on

A faulty lid switch or door lock, a failed control board, a blown thermal fuse or a timer fault can stop the cycle before it begins.

Door won’t unlock

Front-load doors stay locked while water remains in the drum. A drain fault or a failed door lock assembly keeps them shut.

Musty or burning smell

Mildew in the door gasket or drain causes musty odors. A burning-rubber smell points to a slipping belt or an overheating motor.

Error code on the display

Codes help narrow the cause, such as a drain, water supply or lock fault. We read them along with a full check of the machine.

When to call

Signs your washer needs a technician now

  • The drum is still full of water at the end of the cycle.
  • Water reaches the floor during fill, wash or spin.
  • You notice a burning-rubber smell or see smoke. Stop the cycle and unplug the washer.
  • Loud banging or grinding during spin, even with a balanced load.
  • The washer trips the breaker or the outlet’s GFCI every time it runs.

Safe checks before you call

  1. Check the breaker and press reset on the outlet if it is a GFCI type.
  2. Make sure the lid or door is fully closed and the child lock is off.
  3. Confirm both water valves behind the washer are open and the hoses aren’t kinked.
  4. Redistribute a heavy or bunched load, then try the spin cycle again.
  5. If your manual shows a front access drain filter, clean it with towels ready for the water.
  6. For musty smells, wipe the door gasket and run an empty hot cycle with washer cleaner.

Stay safe: unplug the washer before checking the drain filter, and don’t remove the cabinet panels or touch wiring. Never move a washer that is full of water.

Top-load, front-load and stacked washers

Top-load and front-load machines fail in different ways. Help your washer last longer

  • Use the right amount of HE detergent; too much leaves residue and causes odors.
  • Don’t overload the drum, and wash bulky items such as comforters on their own.
  • Leave the front-load door open between washes so the gasket can dry.
  • Empty pockets: coins and hair pins are a common cause of pump damage.
  • Replace rubber fill hoses that look cracked or bulged.
