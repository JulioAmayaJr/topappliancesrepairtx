If your oven will not heat while the cooktop burners still work, the fault is in the oven circuit rather than the appliance as a whole. On a gas range the cause is usually a weak oven igniter that no longer draws enough current to open the gas safety valve. On an electric oven it is normally a broken bake element or a drifting temperature sensor. Both are standard repairs, and both are easy to confirm.

Why an oven will not heat

Gas and electric ovens fail in different ways, so start with the type you have.

1. A weak oven igniter (gas ovens)

This is the single most common gas oven fault. The igniter glows to heat a bimetal safety valve, which then releases gas. As the igniter ages it still glows orange but draws less current, and eventually the valve never opens. The telltale sign is an igniter that glows for several minutes with no flame.

2. A broken bake element (electric ovens)

The element is the visible loop at the bottom of the cavity. It blisters, sags and finally splits. Sometimes you can see the break; often you cannot, and it is confirmed with a continuity test. The broil element can fail the same way while bake still works, and vice versa.

3. A failed oven temperature sensor

The sensor is the thin probe inside the cavity that reports temperature to the control board. When it drifts, the oven heats to the wrong temperature, cycles strangely or refuses to start heating at all. Ovens that bake unevenly usually trace back here.

4. A failed control board or thermostat

The board decides when to energise the element or igniter circuit. A failed relay on the board produces an oven that is completely dead while the display works normally.

5. A tripped breaker or a lost leg of power (electric)

An electric range runs on a 240-volt double breaker. If one leg trips, the cooktop elements may still work on 120 volts while the oven gets nothing — which fools people into thinking the oven itself failed.

6. A safety valve that has failed (gas ovens)

Less common than the igniter, but when a good igniter still does not open the valve, the valve is the remaining part in that circuit.

What you can safely check yourself

  1. Confirm the oven is not in Sabbath mode, delay start, or a locked self-clean cycle — all three prevent normal heating.
  2. On an electric range, switch the double breaker fully off and back on in case one leg has tripped.
  3. On a gas range, confirm the cooktop burners light. If they do, gas is reaching the appliance and the oven circuit is the problem.
  4. Watch a gas oven through the window for about four minutes: if the igniter glows but no flame appears, that points straight at the igniter.
  5. On an electric oven, look at the bake element while it should be heating — a working element glows evenly orange.
  6. Check the oven temperature with a separate oven thermometer before assuming the oven is dead; it may be heating to the wrong temperature instead.

Stay safe: do not attempt to replace a gas oven igniter or safety valve yourself — the repair involves the gas supply and a leak check afterwards. If you smell gas at any point, do not operate any switch: leave the house and call your gas company or 911 from outside. On electric ranges, switch the breaker off before touching an element; the circuit carries 240 volts even when the oven appears dead.

When to call a technician

  • A gas oven igniter glows for minutes and the burner never lights.
  • The bake element does not glow, or glows only in part of its length.
  • The oven heats to the wrong temperature or will not hold a setting.
  • The oven is completely dead while the display and the cooktop work.
  • You smell gas, or you see scorch marks or sparking inside the cavity.

How we fix an oven that will not heat

The technician tests the oven circuit live instead of guessing. On a gas oven we measure the current the igniter actually draws — a glowing igniter can still be a failed one, and the current reading is what separates an igniter fault from a valve fault. On an electric oven we check continuity through the elements, the resistance of the temperature sensor and the relay output of the control board.

Any gas repair finishes with a leak check at the connection before we leave, and the oven is run up to temperature and verified with a thermometer rather than the display.
