Common problems

Common refrigerator problems we repair

These are the refrigerator problems Houston homeowners call us about most, and what is usually behind them.

Refrigerator not cooling

Usually a stopped evaporator or condenser fan, dusty condenser coils, a failed start relay or a defrost fault that has iced over the evaporator.

Water leaking on the floor

Most often a clogged defrost drain, a cracked drain pan, or a loose or split water line feeding the ice maker and dispenser.

Ice building up in the freezer

Frost on the back wall points to the defrost system: heater, defrost thermostat or control board. A torn door gasket that lets humid air in does the same.

Noisy or clicking compressor

Clicking every few minutes often means the start relay or overload is failing. Grinding or buzzing can come from a fan motor or the compressor itself.

Ice maker not making ice

A frozen fill tube, a failed water inlet valve, a clogged filter or a worn ice maker module are the usual causes.

Fan not running

When a fan stops, the fresh-food side warms up while the freezer may still feel cold. Worn motors and blades blocked by ice are common.

Runs all the time

Dirty coils, a weak door seal, a failing temperature sensor or a sealed-system problem keep the compressor running without a break.

Lights, display or controls dead

If the outlet has power, the fault is usually a control board, a door switch or a wiring connection inside the cabinet.

When to call

Signs your refrigerator needs a technician now

  • Food in the fresh-food section feels warm or the thermometer reads above 40°F.
  • Water keeps pooling under the refrigerator or inside the crisper drawers.
  • The compressor clicks on and off but never keeps running.
  • You smell something burning or see scorch marks near the back. Unplug it first.
  • The freezer back wall is covered in thick frost and the fridge is warming up.

Safe checks before you call

  1. Plug a lamp into the same outlet to confirm it has power, and check the breaker.
  2. Make sure the temperature controls are set around 37°F for the fridge and 0°F for the freezer.
  3. Close the door on a sheet of paper: if it slides out easily, the gasket may not be sealing.
  4. Move food that is blocking the air vents inside the freezer and fresh-food sections.
  5. If ice or water stopped, check whether the water filter is past its replacement date.
  6. If the coils sit behind a front grille, vacuum the dust gently without moving the refrigerator.

Stay safe: don’t open rear panels, touch wiring or try to handle refrigerant lines. If you smell burning, unplug the refrigerator and call us.

Repair or replace your refrigerator?

There is no single rule. The answer depends on the age of the refrigerator, which part failed and what the repair costs compared with the value of the unit. A failed fan motor, relay or defrost part is usually worth fixing; a major sealed-system failure on an older, inexpensive model may not be. Simple habits that keep a refrigerator running

  • Vacuum the condenser coils a couple of times a year, more often if you have pets.
  • Leave a few inches of space around the cabinet so heat can escape.
  • Wipe door gaskets with warm soapy water so they keep sealing.
  • Change the water filter on schedule if you have an ice maker or dispenser.
  • Avoid packing shelves so tightly that cold air can’t circulate.
