Common problems

Common stove and oven problems we repair

Gas or electric, freestanding or built in, these are the stove and oven problems we repair most often.

Oven not heating

On gas ovens, a weak igniter is the most common cause. On electric ovens, a burned-out bake or broil element, a sensor or a control board.

Wrong oven temperature

A failing temperature sensor, a worn igniter or a control that needs calibration makes food burn or stay raw.

Gas burner won’t light

A dirty or misaligned burner cap, a cracked igniter or a faulty spark module keeps the burner from lighting.

Electric burner not heating

A failed surface element, a loose terminal block or a bad infinite switch stops one burner while the others work.

Igniter clicks nonstop

Moisture after cleaning or a failed spark switch keeps the igniters clicking even with the knobs off.

Door locked after self-clean

The door lock motor or switch can fail during the high heat of a self-clean cycle and keep the door shut.

Self-clean doesn’t finish

A failed lock, sensor or control board stops the cycle early or keeps it from starting.

Error code or dead control panel

Codes usually point to a sensor, lock or board fault. A dead panel can be a power supply or control problem.

When to call

Signs your stove or oven needs a technician now

  • An element sparks, glows unevenly or has a visible blister.
  • A gas burner clicks but doesn’t light, or the flame is yellow and lazy instead of blue.
  • The oven door stays locked after a self-clean cycle has cooled down.
  • Food burns or stays undercooked at the usual time and temperature.
  • The range trips the breaker when you turn on the oven or a burner.

If you smell gas: don’t turn on lights, burners or anything electric. Leave the house, then call your gas company’s emergency line or 911 from outside. Call us after the gas company says it is safe.

Safe checks before you call

  1. Electric range: check the breaker; ranges often use a double breaker.
  2. Gas range: if one burner won’t light, see whether the others do.
  3. After cleaning, make sure burner caps and heads are dry and seated correctly.
  4. Look for a control lock, Sabbath mode or delay start on the oven display.
  5. Use an inexpensive oven thermometer to see how far off the temperature really is.
  6. After self-clean, let the oven cool completely before trying to open the door.

Stay safe: don’t remove panels, disconnect gas lines or work on wiring. Turn off the burner or oven and call us.

Keep your stove and oven in good shape

  • Wipe spills on the cooktop once it cools so they don’t bake onto burners and igniters.
  • Let burner parts dry fully after washing before you light them.
  • Use self-clean sparingly; frequent cycles stress door locks and sensors.
  • Don’t cover the oven floor with foil, which can block heat and damage the finish.
  • Keep the area around the oven vent clear.
