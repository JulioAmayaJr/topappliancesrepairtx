If there is still water in the bottom of your dishwasher when the cycle ends, something in the drain path is blocked or the drain pump is not running. The most common causes, in order, are a clogged filter, food debris in the drain hose or the pump itself, a blocked air gap at the sink, and a garbage disposal whose knockout plug was never removed. Most of these are quick repairs.

Why water stays in the bottom of a dishwasher

The drain path runs from the sump, through the filter and pump, along the drain hose and into the disposal or sink drain. A blockage anywhere along it produces the same puddle.

1. A clogged filter and sump

Modern dishwashers have a removable cylindrical filter at the bottom. Labels, seeds, broken glass and grease build up there until water cannot reach the pump. This is the first thing to check and the most common cause by a wide margin.

2. Food debris jamming the drain pump

Small items get past the filter and lodge in the pump impeller. The pump hums or stays silent, and nothing drains. Clearing it is a standard repair.

3. A kinked or clogged drain hose

The hose from the dishwasher to the sink cabinet gets kinked when the machine is pushed back into place, or clogs with grease over the years. Either stops the flow even when the pump works perfectly.

4. A blocked air gap

If your sink has a small chrome cylinder on the deck, that is the air gap. It clogs with debris and backs water up into the dishwasher. Cleaning it is easy once you know it exists.

5. The garbage disposal knockout plug

When a dishwasher is connected to a new disposal, the knockout plug inside the disposal inlet must be removed. If an installer missed it, the dishwasher will never drain — and the symptom appears right after new appliance work.

6. A failed drain pump or control fault

If the pump is clear and still does not run, the pump motor or the board driving it has failed. This is the least common cause, and the technician confirms it by testing rather than assuming.

What you can safely check yourself

  1. Turn the dishwasher off at the breaker before putting your hands inside it.
  2. Scoop the standing water out with a cup and a sponge so you can see the bottom of the tub.
  3. Twist out the cylindrical filter, rinse it under hot water and clear the sump area underneath, watching for broken glass.
  4. If your sink has an air gap, lift the cover and clean out any debris inside.
  5. Look under the sink and straighten any kink in the dishwasher drain hose.
  6. Run the garbage disposal for 20 seconds, then start a rinse cycle and see whether the water clears.

Stay safe: switch the dishwasher off at the breaker before reaching into the sump, and use a towel rather than bare fingers — broken glass hides in the filter area. Do not pour drain cleaner into a dishwasher: it damages seals and plastic parts and does not reach the blockage.

When to call a technician

  • The filter, hose and air gap are all clear and water still stands in the tub.
  • The dishwasher hums during the drain phase but nothing moves.
  • Water backs up into the sink when the dishwasher drains.
  • The problem started right after a new garbage disposal was installed.
  • There is water on the floor under the machine as well as inside it.
