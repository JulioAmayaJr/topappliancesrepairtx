When an ice maker stops making ice, the cause is almost always one of four things: no water reaching it, a fill tube frozen solid, a freezer that is not cold enough, or a worn-out ice maker module. In Houston we also see ice makers that simply cannot keep up in August, which is a different problem from a broken one. Here is how to tell which you have.

Why an ice maker stops making ice

Work through these in order — the first three account for most of the calls we take.

1. A frozen fill tube

The small tube that delivers water into the ice mold freezes shut when the freezer runs colder than normal or when a slow leak drips and refreezes. No water gets in, so no ice comes out, and the ice maker keeps cycling as if nothing is wrong.

2. A failed water inlet valve

This electrically operated valve opens to fill the mold. When it fails, or when the water line feeding it is kinked or shut off, the ice maker runs dry. A valve that fails partly open causes the opposite symptom: ice that overflows the mold.

3. The shut-off arm or sensor is in the stop position

Every ice maker has a way of knowing the bin is full — a wire arm on older units, an infrared sensor on newer ones. If the arm has been knocked up, or something in the bin blocks the sensor, the ice maker stops on purpose. This takes ten seconds to check and is more common than people expect.

4. The freezer is not cold enough

Ice makers need roughly 0 to 5 °F to cycle. If the freezer has drifted warmer because of a dirty condenser, a failing evaporator fan or a door left ajar, ice production slows and then stops. Fixing the ice maker will not help if the real issue is the freezer.

5. A worn ice maker module

The module contains the motor, gears and thermostat that run the harvest cycle. After years of service the gears strip or the thermostat fails, and the unit stops ejecting ice. This is a standard replacement part.

6. A clogged water filter

A filter that is past due restricts flow enough that the mold never fills properly. Cubes come out small, hollow or not at all. Filters are designed to be changed roughly every six months.

What you can safely check yourself

  1. Check that the shut-off arm is in the down position, or that nothing is blocking the sensor eye in the bin.
  2. Put a freezer thermometer in the compartment and confirm it reads 0 to 5 °F.
  3. Check the water shut-off valve behind or under the refrigerator is fully open and the line is not kinked.
  4. Replace the water filter if it has been more than six months, then discard the first few batches of ice.
  5. Use the water dispenser if your model has one — if water flows normally, the supply side is probably fine.
  6. Look for a visible plug of ice at the fill tube above the ice mold.

Stay safe: unplug the refrigerator before reaching behind the ice maker, and never use a knife, screwdriver or hot water to chip ice out of the mold or the fill tube. Puncturing the mold or an evaporator line turns a small repair into a major one. A hair dryer on a low setting, used briefly and from a distance, is the safe way to thaw a fill tube.

When to call a technician

  • The fill tube refreezes within days of being thawed.
  • No water reaches the ice maker but the dispenser works normally.
  • The freezer cannot hold 0 to 5 °F.
  • The ice maker cycles and ejects, but the cubes are hollow, small or stuck together.
  • You hear a grinding noise from the ice maker during the harvest cycle.

How we fix an ice maker that stopped producing

The technician separates the three systems involved: water supply, freezer temperature and the ice maker itself. We measure the water flow at the valve, check the actual compartment temperature rather than the display, and test the module through a harvest cycle. That tells us which of the three is at fault instead of replacing parts in sequence.

When the fill tube is frozen, we thaw it and then find out why it froze, because a tube that refreezes points at a leaking valve or an airflow problem that will bring you right back to the same symptom.
