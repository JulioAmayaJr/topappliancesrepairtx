Common problems

Common microwave problems we repair

These are the microwave faults we see most in Houston kitchens — most of them point to a short list of parts.

Runs but doesn’t heat

The light is on and the turntable spins, but food stays cold. Usually a failed magnetron, high-voltage diode or capacitor, or a faulty door switch.

Sparking or arcing inside

A worn waveguide cover, chipped rack supports or burned spots in the cavity. Stop using the microwave until it is repaired.

Dead display or no power

A blown internal fuse, a failed door switch chain or a control board fault can leave the microwave completely dark.

Stops mid-cycle

Overheating, an intermittent door switch or a failing control board can cut the cycle short or restart it at random.

Turntable won’t spin

A worn turntable motor or a broken drive coupling leaves food heating unevenly — hot at the edge, frozen in the middle.

Loud buzzing or humming

A failing magnetron, a bad high-voltage diode or a worn cooling fan motor makes a healthy hum turn into a harsh buzz.

Door won’t latch or unlock

Broken latch hooks or a misaligned door keep the interlock switches open, so the microwave refuses to start at all.

Vent fan or light problems (over-the-range)

On over-the-range models the exhaust fan, charcoal filter and cooktop light are part of the unit — all repairable without replacing it.

When to call

Signs your microwave needs a technician now

  • Sparks, arcing or a burning smell during use. Stop using it immediately.
  • Food comes out cold even though the microwave runs a full cycle.
  • The microwave runs with the door open, or starts by itself. Unplug it.
  • Loud buzzing that wasn’t there before, or the breaker trips when it runs.
  • A built-in or over-the-range unit with a dead display or failing keypad.

Safe checks before you call

  1. Check the breaker, or try another small appliance in the same outlet.
  2. Make sure the door closes cleanly and nothing blocks the latch.
  3. Confirm the child lock isn’t on (many models lock the keypad after holding a key).
  4. Remove any metal — foil, twist ties, gold-rimmed plates — and clean food residue off the cavity walls.
  5. Never run the microwave empty; it damages the magnetron.

Stay safe: never open a microwave’s cabinet. The high-voltage capacitor holds a dangerous charge even when the unit has been unplugged for days. Microwave repair is strictly a job for a technician with the tools to discharge it.
