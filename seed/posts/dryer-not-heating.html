If your dryer runs a full cycle but the clothes come out cold and damp, the drum motor is fine and something in the heating circuit has stopped. On an electric dryer the usual culprits are a blown thermal fuse, a burned-out heating element or a failed thermostat. On a gas dryer it is almost always the igniter or the gas valve coils. A blocked vent causes the same complaint and is the single most common cause we find in Houston homes.

Why a dryer runs but does not heat

These are the causes we find most often, in roughly the order they turn up on a service call.

1. A blocked or crushed vent duct

This is the first thing to rule out, because it is the most common and it masquerades as every other fault. When lint packs the duct, moist air cannot leave the drum, so clothes stay damp and the dryer overheats. Many dryers then shut the heat off to protect themselves, which looks exactly like a failed element. A crushed flexible duct behind the machine does the same thing.

2. A blown thermal fuse

The thermal fuse is a one-time safety device that opens when the dryer overheats, usually because of that blocked vent. Once it blows, the dryer will still tumble on most models but will never heat again. Replacing the fuse without clearing the airflow problem just blows the next one.

3. A failed heating element (electric dryers)

The element is a coil of resistance wire inside a housing. It sags and eventually breaks with age. A broken element gives you a dryer that tumbles, lights up and times out with cold clothes. It is a standard, affordable replacement.

4. A weak igniter or failed gas valve coils (gas dryers)

On a gas dryer the igniter glows to light the burner, and the valve coils open to let gas through. A weakening igniter still glows but no longer draws enough current to open the valve, so the burner never lights. Failed coils give the same symptom. Both are routine parts.

5. A failed cycling thermostat or thermistor

The thermostat tells the dryer when to cut and restore heat; the thermistor reports drum temperature to the control board. When either drifts or fails, the dryer may heat weakly, briefly or not at all.

6. A tripped half of the breaker (electric dryers only)

An electric dryer uses a double 240-volt breaker. If one leg trips, the drum still turns on 120 volts but the heating element gets nothing. This is the one cause you can often fix yourself in two minutes.

What you can safely check yourself

  1. Check the breaker panel. On an electric dryer, switch the double breaker fully off and back on — one leg can trip while the drum keeps turning.
  2. Clean the lint screen, then pull the dryer out and make sure the duct behind it is not crushed against the wall.
  3. Run the dryer and step outside to the vent hood: you should feel a steady flow of warm air and see the flap open.
  4. Try a smaller load. An overstuffed drum cannot tumble or dry evenly and will mimic a heating fault.
  5. On a gas dryer, confirm the gas supply valve behind the machine is open and that other gas appliances in the house are working.

Stay safe: never run a dryer that smells like burning, and unplug it before touching anything behind the panels. Lint is fuel, and the heating circuit on an electric dryer carries 240 volts. If you smell gas near a gas dryer, do not touch any switch — leave the house and call your gas company or 911 from outside.

When to call a technician

  • The breaker is on, the vent is clear and the dryer still runs cold.
  • The thermal fuse has been replaced before and blew again.
  • A gas dryer clicks or glows but the burner never lights.
  • Clothes smell scorched, or you see smoke or burn marks on the drum.
  • The dryer heats for a few minutes and then quits every cycle.

How we fix a dryer that will not heat

A technician tests the heating circuit live rather than swapping parts: supply voltage at the terminal block, continuity through the element or igniter, the thermal fuse, the cycling thermostat and the thermistor. On a gas dryer we watch the igniter glow and time how long before the valve opens, which tells us immediately whether the igniter or the coils are at fault.

Airflow is measured as part of the same visit, because fixing a blown fuse without clearing the duct guarantees a repeat call. If the duct is the problem, our dryer vent cleaning service clears the whole run to the exterior hood.
