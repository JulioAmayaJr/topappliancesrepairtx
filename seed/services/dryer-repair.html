Common problems

Common dryer problems we repair

From a drum that spins cold to a dryer that quits mid-cycle, these are the faults Houston homeowners call us about most.

Runs but no heat

On electric dryers, usually a burned heating element, a blown thermal fuse or a failed thermostat. On gas dryers, the igniter or the gas valve coils.

Takes too long to dry

Almost always a lint-clogged or crushed vent duct that traps moist air, or a failed moisture sensor ending the cycle too early.

Drum won’t turn

A snapped drive belt is the classic cause; a worn idler pulley, drum rollers or a seized motor produce the same symptom with more noise first.

Won’t start

A failed door switch, start switch or thermal fuse, or a control board fault, can keep the dryer completely dead even with power at the outlet.

Squealing, thumping or grinding

Worn drum rollers, a dry idler pulley or failing drum bearings get louder with every load until the drum stops turning.

Shuts off mid-cycle

Overheating from a blocked vent, a weak cycling thermostat or a struggling motor makes the dryer protect itself and stop early.

Burning smell

Lint built up around the element or burner, a slipping belt or a seizing motor. Stop the dryer and unplug it — lint is fuel for a fire.

Error code on the display

Codes narrow the fault to airflow, heat or sensors. We read them along with a full check of the machine and the vent path.

When to call

Signs your dryer needs a technician now

  • Clothes come out cold and damp after a full cycle.
  • You smell burning or see scorch marks on clothes. Stop and unplug the dryer.
  • Loads that used to take one cycle now take two or three.
  • The drum squeals or thumps even with a small load.
  • The dryer trips the breaker, or a gas dryer’s flame keeps cutting out.

Safe checks before you call

  1. Check the breaker — electric dryers use a double breaker, and one leg can trip alone, which leaves the drum turning with no heat.
  2. Clean the lint screen and make sure the duct behind the dryer isn’t crushed against the wall.
  3. Step outside while the dryer runs: the vent flap should open with a steady flow of warm air.
  4. Dry smaller loads — an overstuffed drum can’t tumble or dry evenly.
  5. Make sure the door clicks fully shut and the child lock is off.

Stay safe: if you ever smell gas near a gas dryer, don’t start it or touch any switch — leave the house and call your gas company or 911. Never run a dryer with a burning smell, and don’t open the cabinet panels yourself.

Is it the dryer or the vent?

Long dry times are the most common dryer complaint in Houston, and in many homes the machine itself is fine: the vent run to the roof or the exterior wall is packed with lint. A blocked vent doubles drying time, overheats the dryer and is a documented fire risk. The technician checks airflow as part of the diagnosis, and when the duct is the problem we offer dryer vent cleaning as its own service.

Make your dryer last longer

  • Clean the lint screen before every load, not after a few.
  • Have the vent duct cleaned when loads start taking longer — about once a year in most homes.
  • Use rigid or semi-rigid metal duct behind the dryer, never crushed plastic hose.
  • Don’t overload the drum; clothes need room to tumble.
  • Dry rubber-backed mats and anything with flammable residue (oil, gasoline, solvents) outside, not in the dryer.
