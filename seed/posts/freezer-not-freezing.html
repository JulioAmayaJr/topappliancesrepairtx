A freezer that hums along but will not hold temperature is usually losing the battle on airflow rather than suffering a dead compressor. In Houston the most common causes are a condenser packed with dust, a stopped evaporator fan, a defrost system that has iced the coil over, and a door seal that lets humid air in. Garage freezers have an extra problem of their own, covered below.

Why a freezer runs but does not freeze

These are the causes we find most often, starting with the one that costs nothing to fix.

1. A condenser packed with dust

The condenser is the radiator that dumps heat out of the cabinet, and in Houston it clogs quickly — faster still in a home with pets. Once airflow across it is restricted, the system cannot reject heat, the compressor runs continuously and the compartment drifts warm. Cleaning it twice a year prevents a large share of the calls we take.

2. A stopped evaporator fan

The evaporator fan moves cold air from the coil through the compartment. When the fan motor seizes, the coil keeps getting cold but that cold never circulates, so the freezer warms up while the compressor runs. A freezer that has gone quiet, or that makes a chirping noise, often has a failing fan.

3. A defrost system failure

Every frost-free freezer melts the frost off its evaporator on a schedule. When the defrost heater, defrost thermostat or the control that runs them fails, frost builds into a solid block of ice over the coil, airflow stops and the freezer loses temperature. Visible ice on the back wall is the classic sign.

4. A worn or dirty door gasket

Houston humidity makes this worse than it sounds. A hardened, torn or sticky gasket lets warm moist air leak in continuously, which raises the temperature, loads the defrost system and frosts everything up. A dollar bill closed in the door should drag when you pull it; if it slides out freely the seal is gone.

5. A garage freezer fighting the ambient temperature

Many freezers are rated for a minimum ambient temperature and struggle when the garage swings between extremes. In a Houston summer a garage freezer can run non-stop and still lose ground. This is a limitation rather than a fault, though a dirty condenser makes it dramatically worse.

6. A sealed-system or compressor fault

The least common cause and the largest repair. If the condenser is clean, the fans run and the coil is not iced, the technician tests the sealed system — and we tell you honestly how that cost compares with replacing the unit.

What you can safely check yourself

  1. Put an appliance thermometer in the freezer and give it a few hours: a freezer should hold around 0 °F.
  2. Pull the unit out and vacuum the condenser coils and the grille, front or back depending on the model.
  3. Listen with the door open and the light switch held in — you should hear the evaporator fan running.
  4. Look at the back wall inside the freezer for a thick build-up of frost or a visible block of ice.
  5. Close the door on a dollar bill and pull: if it slides out with no resistance, the gasket needs attention.
  6. Make sure nothing is blocking the air vents inside the compartment and that the door closes fully.

Stay safe: unplug the freezer before cleaning the condenser or reaching behind any interior panel, and never chip ice off an evaporator coil with a knife or screwdriver — puncturing a refrigerant line converts an affordable repair into a replacement. If you need to defrost manually, let it melt or use a hair dryer on a low setting at a distance.

When to call a technician

  • Frost rebuilds on the back wall within days of defrosting it.
  • The compressor runs constantly and the compartment still sits above 10 °F.
  • The evaporator fan is silent or makes a loud chirping or grinding noise.
  • The condenser is clean, the fans run and the temperature is still wrong.
  • Food is thawing and refreezing, leaving ice crystals on everything.

How we fix a freezer that will not freeze

The technician separates the three systems that can cause this: heat rejection, air circulation and defrost. We measure the actual compartment and coil temperatures, confirm the condenser and evaporator fans run, and check whether the defrost cycle is completing. That isolates the cause instead of replacing parts in sequence.

If the coil is iced, we find out why it iced rather than just melting it — a heater, a thermostat, a control or a door seal letting humid air in. Clearing the ice without fixing the cause brings you right back to the same symptom in a couple of weeks.
