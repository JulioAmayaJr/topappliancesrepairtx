If your Bosch dishwasher leaves dishes wet, the first thing to know is that most Bosch models have no exposed heating element to bake the water off. They dry by condensation: the final rinse heats the dishes, and the moisture then condenses on the cooler stainless steel tub wall and drains away. That design is why Bosch dishwashers are quiet and energy efficient, and it is also why plastics stay damp and why rinse aid matters far more than on other brands.

Why a Bosch dishwasher leaves dishes wet

Start by separating normal condensation-drying behaviour from an actual fault.

1. No rinse aid, or the dispenser is set too low

Rinse aid is not optional on a condensation-drying dishwasher. It breaks the surface tension so water sheets off instead of beading, and without it dishes come out spotted and wet even when everything is working. If drying got worse gradually, check this first.

2. Plastic items simply do not hold heat

Condensation drying relies on dishes retaining heat from the final rinse. Glass and ceramic hold that heat well; plastic does not, so plastic containers come out damp even on a perfectly healthy machine. This is a characteristic of the design, not a fault.

3. The door was not opened at the end of the cycle

Many Bosch models release the door slightly at the end to let steam escape. If that is disabled, or the dishwasher is unloaded immediately, moisture stays in the tub and settles back on the dishes.

4. A failed vent or vent flap

The vent lets humid air out of the tub in a controlled way. When the flap sticks shut or its actuator fails, moisture cannot leave and everything stays wet. This is a genuine fault and a standard repair.

5. The water is not getting hot enough

Condensation drying depends on a hot final rinse. If the heating circuit has failed, or the incoming water is too cool, the dishes never get hot enough to dry themselves. The same fault often shows up as poorer cleaning too.

6. An overloaded or badly loaded rack

Dishes that nest together or block each other trap water. Cups and bowls left flat collect a pool that no drying system can remove.

What you can safely check yourself

  1. Open the rinse aid dispenser and refill it; if it is empty, run a cycle and judge the result before anything else.
  2. Turn the rinse aid setting up a level or two — the adjustment is inside the dispenser on most models.
  3. Run the hot tap at the kitchen sink until it is hot before starting the dishwasher, so the first fill is not cold water.
  4. Leave the door ajar for 20 to 30 minutes after the cycle finishes rather than unloading immediately.
  5. Reload the bottom rack so cups and bowls are angled rather than flat, and nothing nests together.
  6. Use the extra-dry or sanitise option if your model has one, which raises the final rinse temperature.

Stay safe: switch the dishwasher off at the breaker before reaching behind the tub or removing any panel. The heating circuit and the control module both carry mains voltage, and the tub holds standing water after a failed cycle.

When to call a technician

  • Rinse aid is full and correctly set and dishes still come out soaking.
  • Glassware and ceramics are wet, not just plastics.
  • Dishes are also coming out dirty or gritty, which suggests the heating circuit.
  • The cycle ends much faster or much slower than it used to.
  • There is an error code on the display or the lights are blinking.

How we fix a Bosch dishwasher that will not dry

The technician starts by confirming whether this is a fault at all, because a large share of these calls come down to rinse aid and loading. Once that is ruled out, we test the final rinse temperature, the heating circuit, the vent flap and its actuator, and read any stored fault codes.

If the heating circuit is the cause, poor drying is usually accompanied by poorer cleaning, and both improve with the same repair. If the vent flap has failed, it is a contained part and a same-visit job.
