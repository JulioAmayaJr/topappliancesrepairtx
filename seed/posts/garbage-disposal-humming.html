If your garbage disposal hums when you switch it on but nothing grinds, the motor is getting power and the grinding plate is jammed. The usual culprits are a bone fragment, a fruit pit, a piece of glass or a utensil that fell in. The important part is what not to do: every extra second you hold that switch is heating a stalled motor, and that is how a jam turns into a replacement.

Why a disposal hums instead of grinding

A hum means power is arriving but the plate cannot turn. These are the reasons, in order of how often we see them.

1. Something hard is jamming the plate

Bone fragments, fruit pits, olive stones, broken glass, bottle caps and small utensils are the usual suspects. They wedge between the impeller and the grind ring and stop the plate dead.

2. Fibrous or starchy waste has bound the chamber

Celery strings, corn husks, artichoke leaves, pasta and rice do not grind so much as wrap and swell. They pack around the plate until it seizes.

3. The motor bearings have seized

On an older unit the bearings can bind after years of use and moisture. The symptom is identical to a jam, but turning the plate by hand will not free it, which tells the technician what has happened.

4. A failed start capacitor

Some disposals use a capacitor to get the motor turning. When it fails, the motor hums but never reaches speed, even with nothing blocking the plate.

5. The unit has reached the end of its life

Disposals are sealed machines with a working life of roughly 8 to 12 years. A motor that hums, trips the reset repeatedly and will not free up is usually telling you it is done — and replacing it costs less than fighting it.

What you can safely check yourself

  1. Switch the disposal off at the wall and leave it off for the whole process. Do not run water or flip the switch again while you work.
  2. Shine a flashlight into the chamber and look for anything obvious wedged against the plate.
  3. Remove whatever you can see using tongs or pliers — never your fingers, even with the power off.
  4. Find the hex socket in the centre of the underside of the unit and work the supplied hex key back and forth to free the plate.
  5. If there is no hex socket, use a broom handle against one of the impeller lugs to rock the plate loose.
  6. Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit, then restore power and test with cold water running.

Stay safe: never put your hand into a disposal, even with the power off and even for a moment. Switch the unit off at the wall before anything else, and if the reset button trips again as soon as you press it, stop and leave it off — a motor that keeps tripping is overheating or shorting and should not be run.

When to call a technician

  • The plate will not free up with a hex key or a broom handle.
  • The reset button trips again within seconds of being pressed.
  • The disposal hums with nothing visible in the chamber.
  • You see water dripping from the body of the unit, not just the connections.
  • There is a burning smell, or the breaker trips when you switch it on.

How we fix a jammed or humming disposal

The technician clears the jam properly, then checks why it stalled: a freed plate that binds again, a motor that draws too much current or a reset that keeps tripping all point at the motor rather than the obstruction. That distinction decides whether the unit is repaired or replaced.

If replacement is the right answer, we can install a new disposal in the same visit, including the drain connections and the dishwasher hose where your sink has one. A blocked shared drain is also a common reason a dishwasher stops draining, so we check that connection while we are under the sink.
